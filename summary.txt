The BBC reported Russia and Ukraine ended US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi agreeing in principle to a large prisoner-of-war exchange, but no breakthrough on ending the war; Zelensky said 1,000 to 1,500 prisoners per side were discussed. Reuters reported leaders opened the COP31 leaders' summit in Antalya, Türkiye, where UN chief António Guterres warned slow action put the world on a path to destruction. The BBC reported US charges against three Iran-backed operatives over 18 alleged plots in the US and Europe, including September's incident at RAF Fairford; Iran denied involvement. Reuters reported DR Congo's Ebola outbreak topped 11,000 confirmed cases, with 5,300-plus deaths in government data, the second-worst on record. CBS News projected Democrat Scott Colom beat Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi's Senate race, the state's first Black senator elected since Reconstruction. Reuters reported Democrats criticised 11 members who missed Tuesday's House vote, where Republicans stalled Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth's impeachment 211-204. The BBC reported El Salvador's claim that its troops and Haitian police destroyed Haiti's 400 Mawozo gang headquarters near Port-au-Prince; reports of up to 20 dead were unconfirmed. Reuters reported a US judge allowed Donald Trump's $10bn defamation suit against the BBC, over a misleadingly edited clip, to proceed; the BBC apologised for the edit but denies defamation. CNBC reported US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, beating forecasts, cementing bets on a December Fed rate rise; AFP said Asian tech shares fell, SoftBank down 9% in Tokyo. Yonhap reported a bus hit 32 vehicles in Seongnam, South Korea, injuring 57 people, nine seriously; the driver claimed brake failure, unconfirmed by police. ABC News reported Australia marked Remembrance Day, with Prime Minister Anthony Albanese laying a wreath in Singapore en route to COP31, while Acting Prime Minister Richard Marles led the Canberra ceremony.
